# TOWERLORDS — Story Bible

*Companion to [TOWERLORDS_ROADMAP.md](TOWERLORDS_ROADMAP.md). Story inspiration: Abe's Oddysee — an ordinary someone inside a machine that eats his kind, carrying hopes far heavier than he is.*

---

## 1. Logline

> A wanderer walks in from the outside world, carrying the hopes of everyone he left behind, to reach the amazing thing at the top of a Tower that feeds on exactly that kind of hope.

The three pillars, fixed by design:

1. **The Wanderer** — not a chosen one. Someone who walked in from outside. The Tower's inhabitants are deadly, but the promise at the top is real.
2. **The tone** — the weight of expectation. Everyone back home believes in you. Hope is real but always *just* out of reach — and the story eventually explains that this is by design.
3. **Delivery** — evolving boss dialogue, rescuable NPCs, environmental lore. No cutscene walls, no pausing the game to read.

## 2. The core trick: the loop IS the story

The single rule that keeps a run-based story from going stale: **never pretend the loop isn't happening. Make the loop canon.**

- Why do you restart at the bottom after death? → The Tower puts you back. A dead climber is worthless to it; a *hoping* climber is food. It doesn't let its meals leave the table.
- Why does the Tower rebuild above the summit (NG+)? → Because the prize must stay one floor out of reach. That's the engine of the whole place.
- Why do bosses remember your deaths (grudges)? → *"The tower remembers"* is already the game's phrase. Everything persistent — grudges, fame, feats, victories — is the Tower's memory, and the story treats it that way.
- Why can friends drop in mid-run (co-op)? → The Tower's song never stops; wanderers answer it at all hours. Another hope walks in. It's canon, no lobby fiction needed.

The game already believes this (`the tower rebuilds`, `the tower remembers`, persistent grudge counters). The bible just says it out loud.

## 3. The world: the Tower and the Promise

**Outside** is an ordinary, struggling world — villages, bad harvests, debts, sick parents. And on the horizon, always, the Tower: taller than weather, older than maps.

The Tower **sings**. Not a sound — a certainty. Everyone who hears it knows, personally and specifically, that *something amazing is at the top, and it is meant for them.* Most people can ignore it. The ones who can't are always the same kind: the ones other people expect something great from. The village's best hunter. The prodigy. The eldest child of a starving family. The Tower doesn't lure the desperate — it lures **the hoped-for**, because they climb carrying everyone else's hope too, and that's a richer meal.

**The truth (revealed in layers, §9):** the Tower feeds on hope. Not death — death is spillage. Its architecture is a hope-farm: floors hard enough to hurt, never hopeless enough to quit; a summit prize that is *real* (this matters — the Promise is not a lie) but a Tower that rebuilds above it; guardians who were once climbers, kept as staff; and a base door that never lets a climber leave *less* hopeful than they came in — because the ones who break get kept (§7).

**The biomes, in-fiction** (all six exist in game):

| Biome | What it is |
|---|---|
| Verdant Vault | The Tower's roots — overgrown, almost gentle. The first taste is free. |
| Ember Foundry | The engine floors, where the Tower builds more of itself. It is *always* under construction. |
| Cryo Archive | Frozen records of every climb ever made. The Tower files everything. You are in there. |
| Bone Crypt | The remains of every failed climb. The Tower doesn't hide its dead — the sight makes survivors climb harder. |
| Void Sanctum | Near the summit the Tower thins. The void here isn't empty; it's the Tower holding its breath. |
| Gilded Halls | The sanctuary floors. The Tower's one mercy — see §3.1. |

### 3.1 Gilded Halls — why the Tower has a sanctuary

Every 5 floors, a safe floor. In-fiction: the Tower *provides* rest stops, and this should feel slightly wrong once you think about it. A farm waters its crops. The Gilded Halls exist so hope can recover before it's harvested again. The Rescued (§7) gather here, and the town hub is canonically the lowest Gilded Hall — the Tower tolerates it for the same reason.

## 4. The Wanderer (the player)

- **No fixed name** — players name themselves (META.playerName). The Tower and its lords call you *climber*; the Rescued call you by name.
- **Backstory in one line, chosen by implication, never a cutscene:** you came from a small place that expected a great thing from you, and the Tower sang to exactly that.
- **The weight:** the Wanderer's defining trait is not strength but *being believed in*. Every rescued NPC, every letter fragment from home (§11), every boss taunt pushes on the same bruise: people are waiting on you.
- **Deaths are canon:** you have died every death in the death log. The Tower rebuilt you at the base — it always does, that's what the flask of the first floor tastes like. The lords know it ("your little lives" — SSYLTH's line, already in game). You are not immortal; you are *kept*.
- **Co-op wanderers** are exactly that: other hoped-for people from other places. Mechanically identical, narratively parallel. The Tower's larder has many shelves.

## 5. The Lords — every boss is what you become if you stop

The Abe's Oddysee move: the machine's cruelest agents are drawn from its victims. **Every lord was a climber.** When a climber's hope finally breaks, the Tower makes an offer: a floor, a title, and never having to climb again. The lords are the ones who said yes. They are not devils — they're quitters with staffing contracts, and each one handles the shame differently. This is why they *remember you* (grudges): you are the thing they used to be, walking past their door every day.

Character sheets — built directly on each lord's existing voice lines:

**VEXROTH, THE WARDEN** *(Act I gate)* — A soldier sent by his city to take the Tower by force, centuries ago. Broke on the first gate he now holds. The lowest lordship there is, and he knows it ("my post remembers its shame"). Secretly, he wants to lose — his death lines are *relief* ("Relieved… of duty… at last"). Arc: across repeated kills, his lines shift from contempt to something like coaching. The first lord to hint the lords were climbers.

**KHARGYLE, THE BOMBARD** *(artillery, jovial)* — A siege engineer whose whole company was consumed floors above him. Took the deal so someone would remember where they fell. The chalk tallies on his barrel aren't kills — they're *names*, and yours is just the newest column. Loud because the alternative is silence.

**MALVESSA, THE SUMMONER** *("children — greet our guest")* — Climbed to demand her children back from the Tower. The Tower gave her children. She stopped checking whether they were hers. The kindest-spoken and most horrifying lord; her arc never states the truth outright, and mustn't.

**TESSERAKT, THE SWEEPER** *(MAINTENANCE COMPLETE)* — So old no one, including Tesserakt, knows if there was ever a person inside. Files an incident report after every one of your deaths. Nobody reads them. Comedy on the surface; the punchline of his final lore fragment is one report, addressed to no one, that begins *"Day one. I will climb it tomorrow."*

**SSYLTH, THE TWIN-FANG** *(the dance)* — A duelist who climbed for glory and found the only opponent that never stays down: the stairs. Took a floor so the duels would come to *him*. Respects you more with every rematch ("you had rhythm"); the closest thing to an honest rival in the Tower.

**AURENOX, THE OVERLORD** *(court and crown)* — A king who climbed so his people wouldn't have to. His people are gone; the court he holds is empty; the executions his fools "beloved" had no audience. His existing death line is the thesis of the whole game: *"A crown… is only… borrowed…"* The lord who most clearly shows the Wanderer's possible future — beloved, expected, hollowed.

**GRIMMOIRE, THE WARLOCK** *(trials, footnotes)* — A scholar who decided that *understanding* the Tower was a loftier summit than reaching the top. Wrong, and unkillably smug about it. You are his longest-running experiment; his grudge lines are peer review. Late fragments reveal his research actually works — he's the in-fiction source for several codex entries.

**NYXARETH, THE VOID-LORD** *(the silence)* — The only guardian who **reached the summit**. Saw what's up there. Came back down, chose the emptiest floor, and has spoken in negations ever since. Nyxareth will not tell you what they saw — the refusal *is* the storytelling. The player's best pre-summit clue that the top is not simple.

**MORVANE, THE REAPER** *(the list, the ledger)* — The Tower's collections department, and the loop made flesh: **Morvane is the one who carries dead climbers back down to the base.** That's why you wake at the bottom. He was the best climber of his generation — the most hoped-for, the one ECHO remembers — who understood before anyone that he would never reach the top, and bargained for the only job in the Tower that never ends. Punctual, polite, and quietly the saddest lord: he has personally delivered every one of your deaths ("I have never missed a collection").

**THE TOWER LORD** *(the summit, floor 50 — no name)* — Not the Tower's master. Its **head of staff**. THE TOWER LORD is a *position*: it goes to whoever conquers the summit and, offered the chair, sits — **and the chair costs you your name.** The one sitting in it now was once the most hoped-for climber of an earlier age; nations expected them to end the Tower. They reached the top, saw the amazing thing, watched the Tower rebuild above it, and sat down. The moveset already tells this story — the Tower Lord *"steals the deadliest move from every guardian below"* because the chair grants the Tower's memory of every climb; the voice speaks as the building itself ("I am every floor you bled on") because occupant and architecture are no longer separable. The existing death line is the whole tragedy: *"At last… someone… to inherit… the view…"* Your victories at the summit are the spine of the endgame revelation ladder (§9).

## 6. Act structure — the five acts already in the game

The acts exist (`ACTS[]`, locked biomes, floor-50 summit). The story lays one question over each act. Act transitions get a **one-line authored beat on first entry ever** (a single subtitle-bar line, `voiceSeen`-gated) — never repeated, never a cutscene.

| Act | Floors | Biome | Existing subtitle | Story question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1–10 | Verdant Vault | *the roots of the tower* | Why is the first stretch so nearly kind? |
| II | 11–20 | Ember Foundry | *the tower's burning heart* | Who keeps building this place — and why is it never finished? |
| III | 21–30 | Cryo Archive | *archives of dead knowledge* | The Tower keeps records. What does it say about *you*? |
| IV | 31–40 | Bone Crypt | *the remains of every failed climb* | The dead are displayed, not hidden. Who benefits from you seeing them? |
| V | 41–50 | Void Sanctum | *the summit approaches* | Nyxareth came back down from up there. Why? |

## 7. The Rescued — Abe's Mudokons, done roguelite

**Mechanic:** rarely (Gold-Goblin-tier rarity), a floor spawns a **Kept One** — a climber the Tower drained to the edge and shelved, held in a sipping-cocoon. Free them mid-combat (they're a lootable objective with light defense pressure) and they appear in the sanctuary from then on. Permanent, account-level (`META.rescued`), visible: **the town literally fills with people you saved.** This is the Abe's Oddysee spine — a persistent, headcount-visible measure of good done across all runs, immune to permadeath.

**Why the Tower keeps them:** a climber who breaks but never *quite* stops hoping is a bottle the Tower can sip forever. The cocoons are its cellar. Every rescue is theft from its pantry — and the lords' dialogue notices at thresholds ("You empty my larder, climber").

**Arc structure:** each Rescued has a 4–5 stage dialogue arc that advances on your *next visits* to the sanctuary (one stage per visit at most, `voiceSeen`-gated), plus small state-reactive variant pools (you died a lot lately / you just won / co-op partner present). Several unlock or re-skin services the game already has, so rescues carry mechanical weight without new systems.

Launch roster (9 — one per lord, thematically):

| Name | Found | Role in town | Expectation facet | Arc in one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WREN** | Act I | None — she's a kid from *your* village who followed you in | The believer. Thinks you can't lose. Knows your death count. | Her faith cracks and mends with your record; the game's emotional barometer. |
| **HALDANE** | Act I | Vendor (re-skin shop) | The investor. "Someone should profit from hope." | Funded a doomed expedition; his cynicism is a bandage — stage 5 reveals whose expedition it was. |
| **SERA-VOSS** | Act II | Gambler (re-skin) | The liar with a conscience. | Sells "maps" of a tower that rebuilds; knows they're lies; keeps selling; slowly stops. |
| **BROTHER COG** | Act II | Companion/egg handler (re-skin) | The optimist. Believes the Tower's creatures can be loved back. | The pet system's canon; his arc is the one *pure* hope in the game — protect it. |
| **MOTTLE** | Act III | Bounty board (re-skin) | The auditor. Catalogs everything wrong with the Tower. | Comic relief whose complaint ledger is, in stage 4, revealed to be a weapon: it's evidence. |
| **GRANNY THORN** | Act III | Flask brewer (re-skin) | The parent. Came to fetch her boy. Still here. | Never says which lord her boy became. Never confirmed. Players will argue about it — good. |
| **FENN & FERRA** | Act IV | Reforge/smith (re-skin) | The pair. Climbed together; one carried the other down. | Two rescues, one arc — the co-op theme made flesh: hope shared halves the weight. |
| **LUMEN** | Act IV | Keeper of the Vigil | The witness. Tends the sanctuary lantern; reads the names of the fallen. | Front-end of the death log: your past runs, spoken as canon. |

## 8. Environmental lore — three channels, all optional

1. **Your own dead.** The death log (last 40 runs) already exists — mark previous death sites on floors with a small cairn: *"You died here. Floor 12. Khargyle's volley."* Cheapest feature in the game, biggest chill. Loot a tiny pittance from your own cairn; the Tower permits sentiment, it's nutritious.
2. **Fragments** (codex, collectible): three authored sets — *Letters from home* (the outside's expectations, addressed to climbers who weren't you), *Staff records* (Tesserakt's reports, Grimmoire's research — the lords' pasts, drip-fed), *Summit accounts* (what earlier climbers swore was at the top; they all differ; they were all right — see §9).
3. **Murals** (per-biome set dressing with one readable line): the Tower paints its own propaganda — climbers ascending gloriously, suspiciously few descending.

## 9. The Summit — the revelation ladder (why victory N ≠ victory N-1)

Story progress is gated to **victories** (`META.victories` exists), not runs. Repetition delivers ambience; *milestones* deliver chapters. Each Tower Lord kill plays the next authored beat (subtitle-bar scene at the summit, ~15 seconds, skippable):

- **V1 — The Amazing Thing.** You win. The summit opens and the promise is TRUE: the view of the entire world at once, the door of light, home visible from here — every player should feel the payoff is real. Then the Tower rebuilds above it (existing NG+ banner), and the nameless lord, dying, laughs: *"I reached it too."*
- **V2 — The Chair.** The Tower Lord's summit greeting changes permanently: THE TOWER LORD is a position, offered to summit-takers — at the cost of a name. They're not fighting to stop you. They're interviewing a replacement.
- **V3 — The Staff.** The lords' origin is stated plainly (Vexroth's and Aurenox's fragments unlock): every guardian said yes to the chair-shaped offer on their own floor. The grudge system reframes — they count your deaths because you're the mirror.
- **V4 — The Farm.** The full mechanism: hope is the crop, the summit is the bait, the rebuild is the harvest cycle, and the song chose you because you were *hoped for*. The expectation that has been the game's emotional weight is revealed as the exact thing the Tower eats.
- **V5 — Nyxareth's Silence.** Nyxareth speaks one full sentence (the only time): what they saw at the top wasn't the prize — it was the *offer*, and the void floor was the only way to keep saying no. Refusing the chair is possible. It just costs everything.
- **V6 — The Offer.** After the kill, the chair is offered to *you*, on-screen, as a real choice. Sitting = a genuine alt-ending (short authored scene; your name enters the lore; NG+ continues with the town's dialogue subtly darkened until you refuse at the next summit). Refusing = the chair-holder's respect, and the setup for V7.
- **V7 — The True Summit.** With the sanctuary populated (rescue-count threshold), the Rescued follow you up. The amazing thing at the top was never the view — it's that the summit is where the Tower is *thinnest*, the one place hope can be handed back out. You open the summit for the people below instead of taking the prize. The Rescued walk out through the door of light. The Tower rebuilds — it always does. The final line belongs to Wren: *"So do we."*
- **V8+ — Endless, canonically.** Post-V7 summits draw from a rich variant pool (the chair's successor cycle, returning Rescued cameos, co-op-aware lines). The story is complete; the climb, canonically, never is.

## 10. The anti-repetition doctrine (binding rules for all story content)

1. **Milestones get authored scenes; repeatables get variant pools.** Never spend a one-shot beat on a moment the player will see 200 times.
2. **Every repeated line is state-keyed.** A line may only ship if some persistent counter (grudges, victories, rescues, streaks, co-op presence, absence time, NG+ depth, hardcore) can change it. The `_pickLine`/`voiceSeen` no-repeat window is already built — extend it, don't bypass it.
3. **The loop is canon.** Any "here we go again" feeling belongs to the *character*, in-world. Lampshade repetition; never apologize for it.
4. **Never pause the game to tell story.** Subtitle bar, banners, ambient lines — the existing delivery stack. A player who ignores every word loses nothing mechanical.
5. **Ration it.** At most one story beat per floor. Silence is pacing; the Tower is quiet the way a farm at night is quiet.
6. **Every line lands cold.** Any given line must work for a player who missed all prior context, and deepen for one who didn't.
7. **Never state Malvessa's truth, Granny Thorn's son, or the Tower's origin.** Held mysteries are renewable fuel; answered ones are spent.

## 11. Co-op canon (drop-in/drop-out, zero friction)

- A friend joining mid-run is *a wanderer answering the song* — an ambient line, no lobby fiction: *"Another hope walks in."*
- Leaving mid-run: *"The song let one go — for now."* No penalty, no story guilt; drop-out must feel as canon as drop-in.
- Lords get small co-op variant pools ("Two of you. The arithmetic of hope is unchanged.") and grudge lines can reference the *other* player's record — the Tower remembers everyone.
- Rescues are shared and credited to all present — co-op must never dilute the game's kindest mechanic.
- Fenn & Ferra are the co-op theme stated in-world; their stage-3 line is the design pitch: *"The Tower feeds on hope. It has never learned what to do when we carry each other's."*

## 12. Sample writing (drop-in ready, matches BOSS_VOICE format)

**The Tower Lord — summit greetings by victory count** (`victories`-keyed variant pools):
- v0 (first meeting): `'So. The one the song sings loudest for. Come — see what you were promised.'` · `'They expect so much of you down there. I remember the weight. Set it down a while — die.'`
- v1+: `'Back for the view? It is still exactly as amazing, and exactly as far.'` · `'You have taken this summit {d} times. The Tower has taken it back {d} times. Notice who is not tired.'`
- v6+ (post-Offer, refused): `'The chair is still warm. The offer does not expire. Mine didn't.'`

**Vexroth — late-grudge softening** (grudge ≥ 8):
- `'{k} deaths at my gate and you still knock. …Lead with the dash this time. I saw nothing.'`
- `'I held this post a hundred years because I stopped. You come back {k} deaths later because you cannot. I no longer know which of us is the prisoner.'`

**Wren — town, state-reactive:**
- after a deep run dies: `'Floor 38! Nobody from home ever — I mean. You'll get it. You always almost do.'`
- after V1: `'You SAW it? What was it like? Why are you making that face?'`
- long absence: `'You were gone so long Lumen almost read your name at the Vigil. Don't you dare.'`

**Fragments — Letters from home (set 1):**
- `'Son — the whole village put in for your boots. Reach the top and wave. We will be watching the horizon. —P.'`
- `'They ask me every market-day if you have reached it yet. I have started lying. Come home. Or reach it. Either. Quickly. —M.'`

**Tesserakt — incident report fragment (final in set):**
- `'MAINTENANCE LOG, DAY ONE. The stairs are dirty. Someone should climb this thing. I will climb it tomorrow.'`
